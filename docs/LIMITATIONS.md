# Vestly Limitations & Regulatory Disclosures

> **Educational Reference Disclosure**: Vestly is a mathematical modeling and financial decision-support tool designed to clarify startup equity compensation. It does not provide certified legal, tax, or investment advice.

---

## 1. Taxation Modeling Assumptions & Boundaries

### 1.1 Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) Under IRC Section 55 & 56
- **Federal Modeling Scope**: Vestly models tentative minimum tax using statutory federal exemption thresholds ($85,700 for Single filers, $133,300 for Married Filing Jointly) and standard 26% / 28% dual-bracket rates with the statutory 25% exemption phaseout.
- **State AMT**: Several states (including California, New York, Iowa, and Minnesota) maintain parallel state-level AMT systems with differing exemptions and rates. Vestly models primary federal AMT exposure with state income tax estimates; individual multi-state apportionment is not computed.
- **Excluded Tax Complexities**: The model does not compute foreign tax credit carryforwards, Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT 3.8% under IRC Section 1411), passive activity loss limitations, or trust income.
- **Minimum Tax Credit (MTC)**: MTC recovery projections assume standard future tax liability exceeding tentative minimum tax and do not model statutory credit limitations across multiple investment partnerships.

### 1.2 Section 83(b) Election Jurisdictional Rules
- **Federal Postmark Rule**: Section 83(b) elections must be delivered or postmarked by the United States Postal Service (USPS) within strict **30 calendar days** from the transfer date of unvested equity under Treas. Reg. § 1.83-2. The IRS has zero statutory authority to grant late filing extensions.
- **State-Level Elections**: Certain states require a separate copy of the Section 83(b) election attached to the state personal income tax return.
- **Capital Risk**: Early exercise requires upfront capital outlay. If the enterprise fails or an employee leaves prior to vesting, repurchased unvested shares cannot recoup the initial cash expenditure or tax paid.

### 1.3 Incentive Stock Options (ISO) Holding Requirements
- **Qualifying vs Disqualifying Dispositions**: Favorable long-term capital gains rates under IRC Section 422 require holding shares for at least **2 years from grant date** AND **1 year from exercise date**.
- **$100,000 Annual Vesting Limitation**: Under IRC Section 422(d), options that vest in a calendar year exceeding $100,000 in aggregate grant-date fair market value automatically convert to Non-Qualified Stock Options (NSOs).

## 2. Private Valuation & Market Data Boundaries

### 2.1 Section 409A Appraisals
- **Independent Valuation Reports**: Common stock fair market value (FMV) in private venture-backed companies is governed by independent appraisals under IRC Section 409A, typically conducted every 12 months or following a material financing round.
- **Common vs Preferred Discount**: Common stock 409A appraisals incorporate discounts for lack of marketability (DLOM) and liquidation preferences, resulting in common valuations typically 20% to 50% below preferred round prices.
- **Sample Data**: Company valuations, funding rounds, and 409A histories displayed in the Research Hub reflect verified sample datasets and secondary market estimates.

### 2.2 Secondary Liquidity & Tender Offers
- **Transfer Restrictions**: Private company common stock is almost universally subject to corporate transfer restrictions, including the company’s Right of First Refusal (ROFR), co-sale agreements, and mandatory board approval.
- **Tender Offer Pricing**: Secondary transactions frequently execute at a 10% to 30% discount to recent preferred financing valuations to compensate buyers for illiquidity.
